# The three DepEd programs — sourcing for Part 6

Researched 2026-08-18. Revision line 35 names three programs and asks for
layman's terms, TED-talk pacing, and flip cards.

Part 6 renders each program as an `analogy` slide — the stage component already
click-to-flips, so no new slide kind is needed. Front is the question a
16-year-old would actually ask. Back is the plain answer in under 40 words,
ending in what it means for *them*.

---

## 1. National Drug Education Program (NDEP)

**Status: fully grounded.** No open questions.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Legal root** | RA 9165 (Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002), Sec. 43, Art. IV |
| **Operationalised by** | DepEd Order No. 12, s. 2009 |
| **Strengthened by** | **DepEd Memorandum No. 200, s. 2016**, "Strengthening the National Drug Education Program in Schools" — issued 23 November 2016, signed by Sec. Leonor Magtolis Briones, through BLSS |
| **Curriculum policy** | **DepEd Order No. 30, s. 2018**, Preventive Drug Education Program Policy — issued 12 July 2018 |
| **Sits inside** | Oplan Kalusugan sa DepEd (OK sa DepEd), DepEd Order No. 28, s. 2018 — pillar 5 of six |

**The line that matters for the room:** DM 200 §1 says school heads are *"fully
responsible for its effective implementation."* Not "encouraged to support" —
responsible. That single clause is why an adviser can commit resources on the
spot and is not doing anyone a favour.

**The twin purpose, verbatim from DM 200:** *create awareness on the ill effects
of abusing drugs* **and** *develop among pupils' and students' positive behavior
and life skills to resist drugs.* The second half is the mandate for everything
this deck does — life skills, not scare-awareness.

**Five components** (DO 12, s. 2009, restated in DM 200): curriculum and
instruction · co-curricular and ancillary services · teacher and staff
development · parent education and community outreach · research, monitoring
and evaluation.

> An SSLG can plausibly own components 2 and 4 outright. Worth saying aloud in
> Part 7 rather than on the Part 6 card.

**The seven directives of DM 200 ¶3** — several are directly ownable by a
student council:

- a. **Establishing Barkada Kontra Droga Chapters in schools**
- b. Intensifying anti-drug abuse information campaigns
- c. Supporting activities of regional and division NDEP coordinators
- d. **Involving PTCAs and pupil/student organizations in prevention activities**
- e. Mobilizing scout leaders via the Drug Abuse Prevention Integrated in Scouting Kit (DISK)
- f. Allocating funds for modules and supplementary materials
- g. Collaborating with LGUs, government units and NGOs locally

**Reporting (¶4):** a **monthly accomplishment report** is due before the 7th of
the following month to the Director, BLSS–School Health Division, DepEd Central
Office, Pasig City. This is the hook for the adviser's sustainability bullet in
Part 7 — the reporting line already exists, so documenting the SSLG's activity
costs nothing new.

**Also worth holding:** DO 30, s. 2018 makes PDE outcomes an **OPCRF performance
objective for school heads** — the strongest single lever in the document — and
its enclosure cites the **Universal Prevention Curriculum (2015)** by name in
its own references. Jessica is teaching the exact curriculum DepEd policy cites.

### Flip card 1

> **Front:** Your school already runs an anti-drug program. Can you name it?
>
> **Back:** NDEP — the National Drug Education Program. Every school has one, and
> your principal is personally accountable for it. The law says it must build
> life skills, not just hand out warnings. Someone has to run the student side.
> That is you.

## 2. EskweLA BAN sa Sigarilyo

**Status: name verified.** The dictation in `CATALYST_FINAL_REVISION.MD`
("Kuwila ban sa sigarilyo") is a real DepEd campaign and the pun is deliberate.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Campaign name** | **EskweLA BAN sa Sigarilyo** — a pun on *eskwelahan*, reading as both "the school's ban on cigarettes" and "the school fights tobacco" |
| **Launched** | August 2017; rolled out nationwide from 2018 |
| **Partner** | Tobacco-Free Kids Action Fund; piloted in Pasig, Makati, Batangas, Bulacan, Bataan, Pampanga |
| **Underlying policy** | **DepEd Order No. 48, s. 2016 — "Policy and Guidelines on Comprehensive Tobacco Control"**, issued **28 June 2016**, signed by Sec. **Br. Armin A. Luistro FSC** |
| **DO 48 references** | DepEd Memorandum No. 230, s. 2011; DepEd Order Nos. 6 and 40, s. 2012 |

**What the campaign is:** an awareness push for the bans already written into DO
48 — it is not itself a separate policy. Say it that way; the room will ask.

**DO 48's three prohibitions**, and the one that hands them leverage:

1. Absolute smoking ban on school premises
2. **No sale or advertisement of tobacco products within a 100-metre perimeter of any school**
3. No tobacco-industry sponsorships or unnecessary interaction with the industry

The 100-metre rule is the usable fact. It is more concrete than RA 11900's
"near schools" and a student can pace it out.

> ⚠️ **Scope limit — do not overstate this on stage.** DO 48 is a 2016
> *tobacco* control policy and **could not be verified as covering e-cigarettes
> or vapes**. It predates the vape surge. The vape half of the argument rests on
> **RA 11900** (Vaporized Nicotine and Non-Nicotine Products Regulation Act,
> 2022): access age 18, sale and use restricted near schools. Pair them on the
> card — DO 48 for cigarettes, RA 11900 for vapes — and do not claim DO 48 bans
> vapes.

**Live development worth a sentence in the note:** DepEd has been working on an
anti-vape memorandum of agreement with the DILG and DTI to clear vapes from
within 100 metres of schools — the same perimeter, extended to the product this
room actually meets. Frame as in progress, not in force.

### Flip card 2

> **Front:** Why is it illegal for a store to sell cigarettes right outside your gate?
>
> **Back:** DepEd Order 48 bans selling or advertising tobacco within 100 metres
> of any school — the campaign is called EskweLA BAN sa Sigarilyo. For vapes, RA
> 11900 does the same. A store near you breaking it is not gossip. It is a
> violation your adviser can report.

## 3. Barkada Kontra Droga (Barkada Kontra Bisyo)

**Status: fully grounded.** Primary source PDF is in the repo at
`deped-issuances/DDB_Board_Regulation_5_s2007_Barkada_Kontra_Droga.pdf`.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Instrument** | **DDB Board Regulation No. 5, s. 2007**, approved **13 November 2007** |
| **Legal root** | RA 9165, Sec. 77, Art. IX |
| **Owner** | Dangerous Drugs Board; flagship programme |
| **Built with** | UNODC financial assistance, as a social-marketing response to amphetamine-type stimulant abuse |
| **Piloted in** | Antipolo, Cainta and Tanay, Rizal |
| **DepEd link** | DM 200, s. 2016 ¶3a directs schools to establish BKD Chapters |

**On the name:** "Barkada Kontra Droga" is the official title in the regulation.
"Barkada Kontra Bisyo" is a **colloquial variant**, not an official rename — it
circulates because the programme in practice covers vices beyond illegal drugs.
Jessica can use it as the familiar name, but the card should carry the official
one. Say "also known as" rather than treating them as two programmes.

**The origin story is the best non-obvious material in Part 6.** The pilot's
finding was that **youth are an effective source and messenger of information
for fellow youth**. That evidence is *why* the programme is peer-led. And the
regulation's own stated aim is to empower the individual to be **"the catalyst
within his peer groups."**

> 🎯 **The deck is named after a phrase in the regulation.** Part 1 defines what
> a catalyst is; Part 6 reveals that the Dangerous Drugs Board used that exact
> word, nineteen years ago, to describe what a student leader is for. The
> finale then hands it to them. Do not spend this in Part 6 — put it in the
> card's `note` and let Jessica decide whether to land it here or hold it for
> the close. It is the single strongest line available to this deck.

**§8.6 — the six Student Council duties** (the spine of Parts 6 and 7):

- a. Encourage students to participate in the BKD programme in their school
- b. Integrate the BKD concept into existing student council programmes
- c. Initiate BKD activities in their schools
- d. **Conduct echo-seminar or training** on prevention and control of dangerous drugs among the student populace
- e. Conduct training in life skills, livelihood and alternative activities
- f. Support the establishment of the BKD Center

Duty **d** is the one held back on the `6` stat slide and revealed on the
`Duty four: conduct an echo-seminar.` statement. It is exactly what a symposium
attendee is meant to go home and do.

**Supporting details for the presenter notes:**

- The **BKD Center is meant to sit in the Student Council's own office** (§7.3a). School officials must supply it. In public schools the NDEP Coordinator is Head Coordinator.
- DepEd's own duty (§8.5g) is to *"Establish a BKD Center in every school"* — advisers can ask this week whether theirs exists.
- Chapters may contact the BKD National Secretariat at DDB **directly** (§9). Students are not stuck going through channels.
- Phase IV includes a **National Search for Outstanding BKD Centers/Chapters** — a ready-made competitive hook for student leaders.

### Flip card 3

> **Front:** Which programme actually puts students in charge?
>
> **Back:** Barkada Kontra Droga. It was built on one finding — young people
> listen to young people. It gives your Student Council six official duties, and
> its centre is supposed to sit in your own office. Nineteen years old, and
> probably news to you.
