# Philippine youth substance data — sourcing for Part 5

Researched 2026-08-18 for `The Catalyst: Youth Leadership for a Drug-Free
School` (SDO Urdaneta City, 21 August 2026).

**Why this file exists.** Part 5 is the credibility act. The deck's entire
strategy is that it is the one talk in this room that did not exaggerate, so
every number on a Part 5 slide has to survive a student googling it on the way
home. Anything below marked UNVERIFIED does not go on a slide.

---

## 1. Corrections to the deck as it stands

Two problems found in `content/catalyst/module-1.ts` before any restructure.

### 1.1 The `423,185` slide is attributed to the wrong survey — **must fix**

The deck currently reads:

```
source: 'Global Youth Tobacco Survey, 2021 and 2023'
```

Those figures are **not** GYTS. They come from the **DOST-FNRI Expanded
National Nutrition Survey**, comparing its 2021 and 2023 rounds. GYTS has no
2021 or 2023 Philippine round — its most recent is 2019.

Correct attribution:

```
source: 'DOST-FNRI, Expanded National Nutrition Survey, 2021 and 2023'
```

This matters more than a footnote. A room that catches one wrong citation
discards the other four numbers with it.

### 1.2 The "1 in 7" slide needs its year

Currently `source: 'Global Youth Tobacco Survey'` with no year. It is **GYTS
2019**. Say the year — a seven-year-old survey stated plainly is credible; a
year-less statistic looks like it is hiding something.

## 2. Verified figures, cleared for the stage

### 2.1 Illegal drug use — the credibility slide

| | |
|---|---|
| **Figure** | **0.1%** of Filipino youth currently use illegal drugs |
| **Slide wording** | `Almost none` / "of Filipino youth use illegal drugs" |
| **Source** | YAFS5 (2021 Young Adult Fertility and Sexuality Study), UP Population Institute |
| **Method** | 10,949 randomly selected youth aged 15–24, 900+ randomly selected barangays, nationally representative |
| **Trend** | Down from 2.4% in 2002, the year RA 9165 passed |

**What this licenses Jessica to say:** that the adults warning them about a
country drowning in shabu were overstating it, and that she is not going to.
This is the sentence that buys the rest of the hour. It is a solid, national,
peer-reviewable number and she can name the sample size out loud if challenged.

**Companion line for the note (spoken, not on screen):** of every 10 young
Filipinos in YAFS5, about **3 drink** (29%), about **1 smokes** (12%), and
close to none uses illegal drugs (0.1%).

> ⚠️ **The "1 smokes" half of that line is now stale — see 2.4.** YAFS5 caught
> smoking at the bottom of a long decline. The 2023 FNRI round shows it turning
> back up. Say the drinking and drugs figures as YAFS5 2021; do not present
> youth smoking as still falling.

### 2.2 Vaping prevalence — the pivot slide

| | |
|---|---|
| **Figure** | **14.1%** current e-cigarette use — roughly **1 in 7** |
| **Slide wording** | `1 in 7` / "Filipinos aged 13–15 who vape right now" |
| **Source** | Global Youth Tobacco Survey **2019**, Philippines |
| **Method** | 6,670 students aged 13–15 |
| **Also from the same round** | **24.6%** had ever tried; **75.5%** were aware of e-cigarettes |
| **Trend** | Ever-use rose 110% in four years — 11.7% (2015) → 24.6% (2019) |

**Explicitly retained by Jessica** (revision line 32). Keep the slide.

**Worth knowing if challenged:** this is the most recent Philippine GYTS round.
There is no 2023 GYTS. Anyone citing a newer *national youth tobacco survey* is
almost certainly quoting the **US** NYTS — see the trap in §4.

### 2.3 The vape explosion — the counter slide

| | |
|---|---|
| **Figures** | **37,513** (2021) → **423,185** (2023) young vape users |
| **Slide wording** | `423,185` / "young vape users in 2023" / context "From 37,513 in 2021." |
| **Source** | **DOST-FNRI, Expanded National Nutrition Survey, 2021 and 2023** |
| **Magnitude** | An ~1,000% rise — roughly **11×** in two years |

**Explicitly retained by Jessica** (revision line 32). Keep the slide, **fix the
source line** per §1.1.

**What this licenses Jessica to say:** "Two years. Eleven times more. Nobody ran
an information campaign about that." Let the counter animation finish before
speaking — the climb is the message.

### 2.4 NEW — adolescent smoking doubled. This strengthens Part 5.

Not currently in the deck. Same survey as §2.3, so it costs no extra
credibility to add.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Figure** | Filipinos aged 10–19 who smoke: **2.3% (2021) → 4.8% (2023)** |
| **Among 18–19 year olds** | nearly **14%** |
| **Vaping among teens who smoke** | **7.5% → nearly 40%** in the same two years |
| **Adults, for contrast** | 18.5% (2021) → 23.2% (2023) — reversing two decades of decline since the Philippines joined the WHO FCTC in 2005 |
| **Source** | DOST-FNRI, Expanded National Nutrition Survey, 2023 round |

**Why this earns a slot.** The deck's pivot is "the thing adults warn you about
is rare; the thing they ignore is in this room." Until now that rested on vaping
alone. This shows the *whole* nicotine picture turning at once, and it closes
the obvious student rebuttal — *"but smoking is going down, my lolo quit"*.
It is also the single most recent national figure available.

**Recommended use:** as `context` on the `1 in 7` slide, or as the spoken line
over the `423,185` counter. Do **not** give it its own slide — Part 5's budget
is 8 slides and three stat slides in a row is already the ceiling.

**No Pangasinan, Urdaneta, or Region I / Ilocos youth substance figure could be
verified.** Public reporting is national only.

This is a real loss. A division-level number would outperform every national
statistic in this room, because it stops being about "Filipino teenagers" and
starts being about the person two seats over.

the division health or guidance coordinator before 21 August could produce a

## 4. Traps — numbers that look right and are not

Anyone re-researching this will hit these. Do not let them onto a slide.

| Claim seen in search | Why it must not be used |
|---|---|
| "2023 National Youth Tobacco Survey: 2.1 million youth, 7.7% currently use e-cigarettes" | This is the **United States** NYTS. It circulates in Philippine coverage without the country attached. Not Philippine data. |
| "11% of students aged 10–15 have tried vapes" | **UNVERIFIED** — no primary source located. Superseded in any case by the GYTS and FNRI figures above. |
| "6.7% of Grades 7–9 students have tried and are using e-cigarettes" | **UNVERIFIED** — no primary source located. |
| "More than 60–70% of students have tried vaping" | A single doctor's unpublished school survey quoted in a March 2026 Manila Bulletin piece. No sample, no method, no schools named. Compelling and unusable. |
| Any GYTS figure dated 2021 or 2023 | There is no such round. The most recent Philippine GYTS is 2019. |
